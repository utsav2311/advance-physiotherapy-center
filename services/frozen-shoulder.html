Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis)

Adhesive Capsulitis, commonly known as Frozen Shoulder, is a painful and restrictive condition where the glenohumeral joint capsule becomes inflamed, fibrotic, and contracted. Patients experience severe pain—especially at night—and progressive inability to lift the arm overhead or reach behind their back. At Advance Physiotherapy Center, Dr. Shahrukh Firoz applies stage-matched clinical interventions, using Maitland passive joint mobilizations, moist heat pre-conditioning, pulley and wand therapy, and rotator cuff re-education to guide patients safely through all three stages of recovery.

Understanding the Condition & Biomechanics

The shoulder is the most mobile ball-and-socket joint in the human body, enclosed by a flexible capsule. In adhesive capsulitis (frequently associated with diabetes, thyroid disorders, or post-immobilization), the capsule develops chronic inflammation and thickens with dense collagen adhesions, severely shrinking intra-articular volume. The condition progresses across three distinct stages: Freezing (painful stage, 2-9 months), Frozen (stiff stage with minimal motion, 4-12 months), and Thawing (gradual recovery stage, 5-24 months). Skilled physical therapy accelerates the thawing phase and prevents permanent joint contracture.

Common Symptoms & Clinical Presentations

  • ⚠️Severe deep shoulder ache, particularly aggravated when sleeping on the affected side
  • ⚠️Inability to perform daily tasks like combing hair, wearing a shirt, or reaching a back pocket
  • ⚠️Marked restriction in external rotation, abduction, and internal rotation
  • ⚠️Compensatory shoulder hiking (shrugging the whole shoulder when attempting to lift the arm)
  • ⚠️Associated tightness and tender trigger points in the upper trapezius and levator scapulae

Evidence-Based Treatment Modalities

  • ✓Maitland Glenohumeral Mobilizations (Grades I-IV): Gentle oscillating glides to stretch contracted capsular folds
  • ✓Pre-Therapy Moist Heat & Ultrasound: Warming deep collagen fibers to increase tissue extensibility before mobilization
  • ✓Codman's Pendulum & Finger Ladder Drills: Gravity-assisted, low-load long-duration stretches for safe joint lubrication
  • ✓Pulleys & Wand Assisted Range-of-Motion: Controlled active-assisted elevation and external rotation exercises
  • ✓Scapulohumeral Rhythm Retraining: Restoring natural 2:1 scapular-to-humeral movement patterns
  • ✓Rotator Cuff & Periscapular Strengthening: Isometric and resistance band conditioning for supraspinatus and infraspinatus

Phased Recovery Pathway

Phase 1: Freezing Stage (Pain Control & Gentle Glides)

Pain gating with TENS, moist heat, Codman's pendulum exercises, and Grade I/II Maitland mobilizations without aggressive force.

Phase 2: Frozen Stage (Capsular Stretching & Mobilization)

Progressive Grade III/IV joint stretching, wand exercises, overhead pulley stretches, and posterior capsular release.

Phase 3: Thawing Stage (Strength & Full Range Restoration)

Rotator cuff strengthening, scapular stabilization with resistance bands, and functional overhead reaching drills.

Phase 4: Return to Full Active Life

Sport-specific or occupational conditioning, posture alignment, and home shoulder maintenance routines.

Frequently Asked Questions

At night, decreased circulation, reduced movement, and increased pressure on the inflamed capsule when lying down exacerbate nerve sensitivity and trigger throbbing discomfort.

Elevated blood sugar levels cause collagen molecules in the shoulder capsule to cross-link through glycosylation, making the capsule unusually stiff, sticky, and prone to severe contracture.

With consistent clinical mobilization and home exercises, significant mobility improvements are typically achieved within 4 to 8 weeks, drastically shortening the natural multi-year disease duration.
